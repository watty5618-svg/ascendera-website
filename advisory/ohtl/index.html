The practice

Transmission work forgives nothing. Neither do we.

Overhead line delivery combines height, stored energy, heavy plant, weather and logistics, often hundreds of kilometres from the nearest town. The margin for error is thin, and the difference between a smooth project and a troubled one is rarely the engineering. It is the quality of planning, supervision and crew competence on the ground. That is where this practice works.

01

Advice from the tower, not the textbook

Every recommendation comes from someone who has erected towers, strung conductors and worked the weather windows himself. Practical, buildable and honest about what crews will actually do.

02

The whole lifecycle, one standard

From route planning and foundations to stringing, energisation and handover, the same person accountable across every phase, so nothing gets lost between stages.

03

Crews that leave stronger

Training and assessment are built into the work, drawing on two decades of the practice lead training crews and building the methods they work to, including adapted training delivered on live projects in the Kingdom. Your crews finish the project more capable than they started it.

What it offers

Six service lines. One accountable lead.

Each can stand alone or combine into full lifecycle support. Every engagement is scoped on evidence and led by the practice lead personally.

Planning and route assessment
Foundations, access, logistics and sequencing, reviewed with a builder's eye. Problems are cheapest on paper, and that is where we catch them.
Tower erection and assembly
Erection methods, supervision structures and lift planning for steelwork at height, from stub setting to final torque, checked against how crews actually work.
Stringing and tensioning
Conductor stringing, sagging and clipping-in: puller and tensioner setups, stringing blocks, crossing plans and the discipline that keeps conductors, and people, safe.
Working at height and rescue
Fall protection systems, climbing discipline and rescue readiness. If someone gets into difficulty up a tower, your crew must be able to get them down. We make sure they can.
Energisation readiness
Pre-energisation inspections, documentation and handover, so the line is provably ready before it goes live, not assumed ready.
Workforce training and assessment
Academy-grade training and competence assessment for linesmen and crews, from new starters to experienced hands, with evidence of what every person can do.

What we deliver with you

Plan the work.
Test the readiness.

Practical support for ultra-high-risk work, grounded in transmission, lifting, work at height and stored-energy exposure. Each engagement is scoped to the hazards and specialist competence required. Authority to release the work stays with the authorised party.

01

Plan the task and its controls

Review the work sequence, interfaces, lifting and work-at-height methods, and the competence and supervision the task requires.

What you receiveReviewed work methods, a responsibility map and a critical-control checklist for the agreed scope.

02

Prepare the crew and rehearse decisions

Use task briefings and practical rehearsal to test communication, challenge, stop-work decisions and rescue readiness before exposure begins.

What you receiveBriefing and exercise materials, competence observations and a clear list of readiness gaps.

03

Support execution and verify readiness

Work alongside supervisors and crews to check the agreed controls, capture changed conditions and follow actions through to review by the responsible authorised person.

What you receiveField observations, readiness records and tracked actions for handover and learning.

The practice lead · Exclusive partnership

Led by someone who has worked the towers.

Lee Stevenson has spent more than two decades in overhead transmission lines, from the tools to the boardroom. He learned the trade on the towers in the UK, erecting steel and stringing conductors in every weather the job can throw at you, and went on to lead crews and advise on live projects across every major high-voltage system, from 132 to 500 kV.

He is the founder, Managing Director and Technical Consultant of Innovecta Ltd, the UK-based OHTL consultancy whose work spans the UK and Ireland, Europe, the Middle East and Central Asia. Through Innovecta he supports utilities, developers and EPC contractors with constructability reviews, delivery support, contractor assessments, incident investigation and training. His Innovecta Technical Library holds more than 455 field-proven OHTL documents: work guides, risk assessments, method statements, toolbox talks and training modules, written from experience rather than theory.

Lee knows this market first hand. His recent delivery support and contractor training includes major high-voltage transmission projects here in the Kingdom, where he has walked the routes, reviewed the methods and trained the crews building them.

Lee works with Ascendera under an exclusive partnership. In the Kingdom, he works only with our clients, on our engagements, and he leads them personally. When he tells you how a crew will behave on a tower, it is because he has been that crew.
