Lee Stevenson leads Ascendera's overhead transmission line practice. His background spans more than twenty years on towers and networks: fourteen years in steel lattice construction, progressing to foreman, followed by maintenance, inspection and technical academy training. He has experience working in the Kingdom of Saudi Arabia and in emerging markets overseas. His qualifications include an NVQ in electricity system technology engineering support, site management safety training and temporary works coordination. That combination brings practical authority to construction methods, crew competence and project delivery.

A transmission programme needs more than a design. It needs methods, crews and sequencing that work on site. Ascendera brings specialist overhead line expertise to systems from thirty three to seven hundred and sixty five kilovolt class, from early planning through delivery.

The practice draws on twenty years of tower and network experience, including construction leadership, maintenance, inspection and technical instruction. Its specialism is modern overhead line technique: the methods, rigging arrangements, equipment and sequencing applied alongside the crews doing the work.

Before mobilisation, we review constructability, access, temporary works and sequencing. For an EPC contractor, this means examining whether the proposed method fits the work, the equipment and the available competence. Technical reviews and capability assessments establish the issues to resolve before delivery.

During delivery, support covers tower assembly and erection, conductor and earthwire works, earthing and bonding, specialist crossings, dismantling, inspection and completion. Engineering and project management support helps teams apply the agreed methodology through complex activities and changing site conditions.

We develop practical programmes for linesmen, supervisors and EPC teams, including extended site support and conversion to specified working practices. Training centre design, curricula and competence frameworks create a consistent basis for development. Assessment checks capability against the work to be performed.

Each activity is supported by a complete document set: a work guide, risk assessment, method statement, toolbox talk, training module and scope sheet. Project-specific documentation and reusable frameworks connect the technical method to instruction, briefing and delivery on site.

The practice supports advanced conductor systems, tower assessment technologies and the introduction of new products and methods. Manufacturer training partnerships and first-use support help prepare the people and documentation. Client representation connects technical requirements with contractors, suppliers and equipment manufacturers.

You can engage retained or ad hoc technical support, capability development, assessment and investigation, project and site support, documentation, or specialist representation. Each engagement defines its deliverables, personnel, dates and acceptance requirements. The route starts with the actual challenge on your transmission programme.
