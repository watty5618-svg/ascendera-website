How it works

Requirements, systems and reality. One continuous improvement cycle.

Step 1

Read your system and identify the gaps

Bring in policies, procedures, forms and operational records. Compare them with the requirements that apply and the evidence from the field, keeping each finding linked to its source.

Step 2

Build a practical response

Prioritise the gaps, draft changes in your organisation’s own format, and prepare role-specific training and actions. Responsible owners review, adopt or reject proposed changes, with their decisions recorded.

Step 3

Verify the changes and keep improving

Check what happens in practice. Feed observations back into the management system, update priorities and give leaders an evidence-based view of progress, unresolved gaps and the next steps.

The architecture

Whichever door you enter, the evidence lands in the same register.

One standard of proof. A check is a check, whether it is a safety barrier, a lifting control or a worker accommodation visit.

One way of scoring. Every product uses the same scale, so you can compare across projects without guesswork.

Set up for you, not rebuilt for you. Your branding, your requirements, your sites, without custom software to maintain.

Your data stays yours. The records, the evidence and the setup belong to you, and you can take them with you at any time.

The confidence model

Every output carries how much you can trust it.

Every finding carries one of four simple bands, so you always know how much weight to put on it. The system applies them automatically, so nobody can upgrade a number because a deadline is near.

Band 01

Not assured

Not checked yet, and shown as not checked, never hidden inside an average.

Band 02

Limited confidence

Based on documents alone, however complete they look. Paperwork can only ever reach this band; proof from the field goes further.

Band 03 · No method ceiling

Reasonable confidence

Checked in person, on site, with a place, a time and a name attached, on documents that are current.

Band 04

Basis degraded

Checked in person, but the document underneath is out of date, so the score is capped until it is refreshed.

Six commitments

Six commitments that make the numbers worth trusting.

They hold when your programme is under pressure, which is precisely when they matter.

Evidence, with the basis attached

We report what we found and how we found it, so assurance stays a live question rather than a framed certificate on the wall.

Delivery and verification stay separate

The people who check the work are never the people who did the work. That separation is written into the contract.

Every signed number is evidenced

For a board pack, a lender or a deadline: if we sign it, we can show you the proof behind it.

Confidence bands hold under pressure

Every score is set by the strength of the evidence behind it, applied automatically, so nobody can negotiate it upward.

Every dashboard opens onto evidence

Every number on a dashboard opens up to the proof behind it. What you see is what was actually checked.

Workers are protected by design

Worker feedback is pooled before anyone sees it, so no individual can be identified. People speak freely, and that is what makes the data worth having.
